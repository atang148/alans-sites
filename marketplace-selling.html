competitive selling marketplace seller's playbook

how to price, list, ship, and operate so your stuff actually sells — at the price it deserves · last updated 2026-09-29 · ← back to alan's sites

overview

Marketplaces don't reward the best product. They reward the seller who prices on sold data, lists so buyers can find it, ships fast and cheap, and protects their seller metrics. Do those four and you beat 90% of casual sellers — whether you're moving enterprise networking gear, a piano, or a drawer of old RAM.

price on sold data, not vibes

Active listings are asking prices — fiction. Sold listings are the market. Before you price anything:

  • eBay sold filter: search your exact item, check "sold items" — that's what buyers actually paid in the last 90 days.
  • Product Research (formerly Terapeak): free inside eBay Seller Hub under Research. Up to 3 years of sales data, average sold price, and sell-through rate — the % of listings that actually sold. A 70%+ sell-through with few competing listings means undersupply: price with confidence. Below 30% means you'll compete on price alone.
  • Median beats average: one outlier sale skews the average. Use the median sold price as your anchor.
  • Match the comps: compare against listings in the same condition, with the same accessories. "New open box" and "for parts" are different markets.

real example: selling an enterprise access point? "Aruba AP-655" as an exact model number in the title sells to buyers searching that part. "Wifi access point, great condition" sells to nobody. Model numbers are the whole game in tech gear.

listings that convert

titles are search queries

eBay gives you 80 characters — spend them like money: brand + exact model + key specs + condition. "HPE Aruba AP-655 WiFi 6E access point, tested" beats "great wifi ap". Front-load the words a buyer would type.

photos do the selling

8–12 photos minimum: front, back, ports, labels/serial plates, and every flaw close up. Daylight or white background. For gear, one photo of it powered on is worth a paragraph of description. Flaw photos build trust and kill "not as described" returns.

fill every item specific

Brand, model, MPN, condition, connectivity — eBay's filters run on item specifics. An unfilled specific is a buyer who filtered you out. Copy the specifics from the best-selling comp listing.

fixed price vs auction

Commodity gear with known sold prices: fixed price, always. Auctions only for genuinely rare items with uncertain value — and start them at the lowest price you'd accept, not $0.99 theater.

shipping without bleeding margin

  • Buy labels outside eBay: Pirate Ship gives you USPS Commercial and deep UPS discounts with no monthly fee and no markup — consistently cheaper than eBay's label rates or the post office counter, especially on heavy gear.
  • Weigh and measure once: a $20 kitchen scale and a tape measure pay for themselves the first time you avoid a postage-due adjustment. Always round up.
  • Free boxes exist: USPS gives away Priority Mail boxes; UPS gives away Express packaging. Order them free instead of buying boxes.
  • Free shipping vs calculated: free shipping converts better — buyers hate surprise shipping at checkout. Price it in: if the sold comps show $120 + $15 shipping, list at $135 free shipping and you'll rank higher.
  • Ship in 1 business day: handling time is a visible metric and a search ranking factor. Print the label the day it sells, drop it the next morning.

real example: a 3 lb access point ships USPS Ground Advantage for roughly half of Priority Mail on Pirate Ship — same 2–5 day window for most zones. Check both before defaulting to Priority.

the seller machine

repricing cadence stale listings rot

Review every listing older than 30 days: drop the price 5–10%, refresh the title, or end and relist to reset search freshness. eBay's Markdown Manager (Seller Hub > Marketing) runs scheduled sales without touching your base price.

promoted listings use surgically

Start at 2–5% ad rate on slow movers only. Never promote your best sellers — they sell on their own and you'd just pay eBay a cut for nothing. Kill any promoted listing that gets clicks but no sales after two weeks.

protect your metrics top rated seller

Defect rate, late shipment rate, and tracking-valid rate decide your search placement and your 10% final-value-fee discount. One "item not as described" case costs more than the refund — photograph flaws, describe honestly, ship on time.

cross-listing one listing, many markets

Tools like Vendoo and List Perfectly push one listing to eBay, Facebook Marketplace, Mercari, and more — with auto-delist when it sells somewhere so you never double-sell. Worth it past ~20 active listings; below that, manual cross-posting of your best items is fine.

returns accept them

Accepting 30-day returns costs you a few return labels a year. Fighting returns costs you defect-rate hits, negative feedback, and hours of messages. The math isn't close — accept returns, price 2% higher to cover it.

mistakes that cost money

The expensive errors are all avoidable. Run this list before every listing goes live.

  • Pricing from active listings instead of sold — you're copying someone else's wishful thinking.
  • One blurry photo. Buyers assume you're hiding damage. They usually are right to.
  • Ignoring Best Offers. A 10%-under offer on a 60-day-old listing is the market talking. Counter once, then take it or leave it.
  • Emotional pricing. What you paid is sunk. The market doesn't care. Price to sell-through, not to "get your money back."
  • Slow shipping. A 3-day handling time on a commodity item hands the sale to the seller shipping tomorrow.
  • VeRO-risk items. Brand owners can pull listings using their trademarks or stock photos fast — and strikes land on your account, not the supplier's. Use your own photos, always.
  • No tracking uploaded. "Delivered" without tracking is a refund waiting to happen.
